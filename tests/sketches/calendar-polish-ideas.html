Sketches · BriefPanel — polish round

Ten ways to make the Tesla calendar feel alive.

Idea 03 (the Tesla touchscreen slab) is in production. This sheet is the polish punch list — fixes for layout edge cases visible in the rendered panel, plus interactions and motion that turn a static card into a professional-feeling instrument. Each idea below is a working live demo on the dark hero ground; click and hover to feel them.

What's already shipped. Pure-black slab, status row with gold dot, 72px weight-200 soonest time, three slabs (first marked active), white CTA, NDA microcopy + serif italic alt link. What still feels static: top row wraps "Filip Z." onto two lines, hover/active states are minimal, the bigtime doesn't react to slab clicks, no countdown signal, no keyboard affordances, no timezone help, no CTA feedback.

Idea 01

Top-row layout fix — stop "Filip Z." wrapping.

Polish · layout edge case

The screenshot shows "FILIP Z." bumped to a second line because the AVAILABLE pill eats the right side of the top row. Fix: make the eyebrow truncate with ellipsis on overflow, lock the status pill to its content size, and on very narrow widths collapse the AVAILABLE label to just the gold dot. Also abbreviate the eyebrow itself — "Next call · with Filip" reads cleaner than "Next private call · Filip Z."

vs. today: visible bug → fixed in one row, no chrome added. The eyebrow becomes a single line at every width and the AVAILABLE indicator degrades gracefully instead of pushing text around.

Idea 02

Live "in 18h" countdown chip.

Motion · relative time

Humans grasp relative time (in 18 hours) before they parse absolute time (14:00 CET). Add a thin breathing dot + relative phrase between the bigmeta and the slabs. Powered by Intl.RelativeTimeFormat; re-renders once a minute. The dot pulses softly so the panel reads as a live instrument, not a screenshot.

vs. today: visitor instantly knows whether the soonest slot is today, tomorrow, or next week — without doing date arithmetic. The "available now" dot also signals real-time presence without adding chrome.

Idea 03

Click-to-select — defer navigation to the CTA.

Interaction · state model

Today every slab is its own link to /contact?slot=… — clicking any slab navigates immediately. That's a hair-trigger commit. Better: clicking a slab updates which one is selected (bigtime + bigmeta + CTA all change in place); only the white CTA actually navigates. Visitors get to scan all three options against the same big display before committing.

vs. today: trades one click savings for a moment of comparison. The bigtime stops being decorative and starts being the answer to "what am I about to book?" Combine with idea 04 for the right physicality.

Idea 04

Animated digit transition — slide and fade.

Motion · physical numerals

Pairs with idea 03. When a slab swaps the bigtime, don't snap — slide the new digit up from the bottom and fade the old one out. Each digit (HH and MM) animates independently, so 14:00 → 09:30 reads as four small dial flips rather than one cut. Total animation: 420ms, eased.

vs. today: the bigtime currently doesn't change at all (it's static). Once it does change with idea 03, an instant swap feels brittle — motion is the proof that the panel is a real instrument.

Idea 05

Slab interactions — corner trace and luminous wash.

Motion · hover state

The current slabs lighten on hover and that's it. Add two flourishes that read as instrumentation: faint gold corner brackets (top-left + bottom-right) that draw in over 250ms, plus a soft radial wash from the top edge that suggests light catching glass. Press state nudges 1px down. Subtle enough that it's seen, not noticed.

vs. today: moves slab hover from "colour change" to "object being touched." The corner brackets are a quiet brand cue (we already use bracket-style ornament in the program-page section openers).

Idea 06

Keyboard-first — 1 / 2 / 3, arrow keys, visible focus.

Accessibility · power users

Bind 1/2/3 to jump to slab N, ←/→ to cycle, Enter to confirm. Mark each slab with a tiny monospace digit in the top-right corner. Add a :focus-visible ring in soft gold. A one-line dashed-rule hint sits under the slabs ("1 · 2 · 3 to choose · Enter to reserve") that's only visible when the panel has focus.

vs. today: nothing keyboard-aware exists. This adds an advisory layer for keyboard and screen-reader users without changing the mouse path. The digit hints also serve as visual anchors during scanning.

Idea 07

Dual-zone time — CET and your local clock.

Polish · international audience

Mobius's audience books from anywhere. Show the CET time as primary, then a short rule and the visitor's local equivalent computed from Intl.DateTimeFormat().resolvedOptions().timeZone. If the visitor is already in CET, hide the second zone. Keeps the bigtime clean and reduces the "let me do mental maths" friction before booking.

vs. today: CET is the only label. Visitors in EST/PST/SGT have to convert in their head before they can decide. The second zone removes that cost without adding chrome.

Idea 08

Capacity meter — "3 of 12 open this week."

Polish · scarcity, transparently

A 12-pip rail under the bigmeta with three filled gold and nine dim. Reads as "Filip is mostly booked but there are three openings." Honest, restrained scarcity — no countdown timers, no "only 1 left!" theatre. Just a quiet acknowledgement that the calendar is real.

vs. today: capacity is invisible. This makes it legible without bragging or pressuring. If the rail ever shows zero open, that's a legitimate signal to use the alt link instead.

Idea 09

CTA states — idle / loading / success.

Motion · commitment feedback

The white "Reserve" button currently navigates instantly. Even a 200ms hold where the label slides up, a spinner takes over, then a check + "Slot held — redirecting" rotates in feels expensive. (In practice the navigation can overlap the success state — the visitor just sees the success blip during transition.)

vs. today: commitment without confirmation feels uncertain. A 600ms loading-then-success vignette is cheap to build, signals that the server received the booking, and gives the visitor a moment of "yes, that happened." Click the button below to feel it.

Idea 10

Mobile reflow — and respect prefers-reduced-motion.

Polish · responsive · accessibility

On a 320px viewport the three side-by-side slabs are too cramped — stack them vertically with the day on the left and the time on the right (a row, not a tile). Drop the bigtime by 10px so the panel fits without scrolling. Foot row becomes two stacked lines. Wrap the digit-flip animation in a @media (prefers-reduced-motion: reduce) block so vestibular-sensitive visitors get an instant swap instead.

vs. today: the panel currently relies on the page's mobile breakpoint to drop the column to full width, but the slab grid stays 3-column and gets cramped. A dedicated stacked layout reads cleanly at any width.
