Sketch · stats-slider-concepts

Ten drafts of the 5-card stats slider.

Shipped today on Home: five cells in a flex scroller with hidden scrollbar, scroll-snap-type: x proximity and a soft right-edge ivory→transparent fade that fades out via .is-end when the user reaches the last card. Works, but the affordance is implicit — the fade is the only signal that there's more. Ten drafts below try different answers to the same question: how does the reader move through five cards?

Each mock holds the data constant (the five live cells) so flipping between drafts feels apples-to-apples. Every draft is real working HTML/CSS — try scrolling, clicking, dragging.

I · Page-turner ledger
Creative

One card at a time. Turn the page.

Cards become pages of a bound ledger — one visible at a time, full editorial breathing room (120 px numerals, 26 px italic tags). A folio number II / V sits top-right of the spread; bottom edge carries ← Previous and Next page → italic-serif buttons. Slide transition is 480 ms. Heaviest visual mass of the ten.

III · Typographic accordion
Super creative

Five cells. One open, four spines.

No scroll at all. The five cells share one fixed-width row; one is expanded at full flex-grow, the other four collapse to 88 px vertical slivers showing only their italic numeral rotated 90° — like a row of book spines on a shelf. Click any sliver to open it. 600 ms ease for the flex transition. Highest "what is this?" moment of the ten.

V · Five pips, nothing else

VI · Ticker marquee
Mine #1

Auto-scrolls right at ticker pace.

The strip moves on its own — 32 s per full loop, constant linear pace, infinite repeat. Pauses on hover and on focus-within. Edge gradients on both sides so cards enter and leave softly. A small italic ⏸ Paused / ▶ Playing control top-right for users who'd rather take control. Financial-paper register — closest mood is a stock ticker.

X · Scrub rail with grip
Mine #5

A draggable hairline rail below the strip.

Ten drafts. The shipped fade is the lightest possible affordance — these explore what happens when you give the slider an actual voice. V if you want the least chrome, IV if you want the strip to feel institutional, II if you want it to feel native to the program-page register, VIII if you want it to feel timeless. The wilder bets — III (accordion) and VI (ticker) — change the whole mechanic.
