Sketches · Navigation · Round 2

The Roman index card, five derivations.

Round one parked on Idea I — the Roman index card: hero ends, the next viewport is a single contents page that lists all nine chapters (About · Investment · Benefits · Eligibility · Documents · Process · Visa-Free Countries · Case Studies · Our Licenses) before any of them open. This file keeps that one and pushes it five ways — each pulling on a different register (editorial, modern grid, type-specimen, audit-report, cinema title-card) without abandoning the contents-page idea.

The constant. Every variant still acts as a contents page — nine chapters, Roman numerals, one click commits a snap-scroll into the corresponding 100vh chapter, and a slim chapter band ("II · INVESTMENT") lives at the top of every chapter view from there on. What changes is the surface, the typography, and the implicit posture of the page — is this dossier a printed newspaper, a product card-deck, a magazine "in this issue", an audit-report front-matter, or a cinema title-card?

Idea I.A — Editorial masthead

The dossier opens like the front page of a paper of record.

The contents card is restaged as a newspaper front-matter page. A heavy ink masthead carries the dossier reference (MOBIUS DOSSIER · No. PRT-GV · 2026.Q1); a gold hairline rules it off; a serif italic deck announces "In this dossier" beneath. The nine chapters fall into the wider column with dotted leaders and tabular page numbers, while a narrower column to the right carries a standfirst — three lines of editorial framing telling the reader what they're about to read and why.

Idea I.B — 3×3 card-deck

The contents page becomes a 3×3 deck — nine clickable cards.

The most "2026 product page" of the five: a clean 3×3 grid of nine cards fills the viewport. Each card carries a Roman numeral, the chapter title, a one-line dek and a doc-ref tag at the foot. Hover lifts the card 4 px and switches its hairline border to gold. The grid renders on cream paper with a slim "Contents · 9 Chapters" eyebrow above and the breadcrumb / dossier code below — same airy proportions as the rest of the brand.

Idea I.C — Type-spec stairway

No card. No grid. Nine italic titles typeset like a magazine "In this issue".

Pure typography. The contents page strips away the card frame entirely and becomes a vertical stairway of nine chapter titles, each set in Cormorant italic at editorial size (32–44 px). Each line is preceded by a tabular Roman numeral and a small-caps gloss; hairline rules space the lines apart. The effect is the front of The Gentlewoman or Apartamento's "In this issue" page — a typographic specimen, not a menu.

Idea I.D — Audit-report contents

The contents page becomes a filing reference — front-matter as audit register.

Reuses the program page's existing doc-control register (the one that prints CATEGORY · DOC-CODE · LAST REVISED) and extends it across a full tabular contents. Five monospace columns — SECTION · CHAPTER · DOC-REF · REVISED · STATUS — list nine rows in Roman order, with hairline gold dividers, a green-dot CURRENT indicator on the active row, and a header block carrying the dossier filename (prt-gv/contents.fm). The most institutional of the five — speaks the same language as a regulatory bundle.

Idea I.E — Cinema marquee black-card

Polarity flips. The contents page becomes an opening title plate.

Inverts everything: ink-black ground, gold ornament, italic chapter titles centred and small-caps tracked. The page reads less like a menu and more like the opening plate of a feature film or the front of a Royal Opera House programme. Three gold pips at the top, the dossier line in small Manrope caps, then nine italic Cormorant titles in a single centred column with Roman-numeral subtitles, ending in a slim BEGIN directive at the bottom. The most creative / cinematic of the five — the one that makes the program look chosen, not browsed.

vs the parent (I.0). Most creative / cinematic — leans hardest on theatre. Where the parent reads as a printed report, this reads as the front of a programme. Risk: it inverts the page palette mid-scroll (cream → ink → cream), so the snap-transition between contents and chapter I needs a soft fade. Strongest emotional register; weakest scannability.

All five derivations share the parent's behavioural contract: hero ends → contents page (page 0) → click any chapter → snap-scroll into the corresponding 100vh panel → slim chapter band ("II · INVESTMENT") at the top of every chapter view from there on. They differ only in the surface of the contents page itself — I.A ink masthead + standfirst, I.B 3×3 card-deck, I.C typographic stairway, I.D tabular audit register, I.E cinema title plate.

Where it lands in the codebase: between <Breadcrumb /> and <section.ht-program> in src/pages/ProgramPage.jsx. Chapter content draws from src/data/programs.js — three new fields are needed (visaFreeCountries, caseStudies, licenses) for chapters VII–IX. Palette tokens are already in public/assets/css/style.css.

Cross-cutting decisions still open: (a) snap-scroll — scroll-snap-type: y mandatory vs. proximity-snap; (b) URL hashes per chapter so deep-links land on the right panel; (c) a prefers-reduced-motion fallback that disables snap and lets free scroll proceed.
