Ten ways to redesign the expanded contents disclosure

The toggle (Contents · 9 chapters · +) is fine. The open state is the problem. Today every chapter eats ~95 px, a dotted leader runs the full column, and the nine entries stack to ~850 px — a print-appendix dropped onto the page, decorative rather than navigational. Ten redesigns below, each holding the same nine anchors, the same toggle, and the same brand register; only the surface of the open state changes.

Behavioural contract — held constant across all ten: the toggle stays as Contents · 9 chapters · +. Click any entry → smooth-scroll to that chapter's id + close the disclosure. All nine entries always rendered when open — no overflow, no inner scrolling. Cream spine background, gold accents, italic Cormorant numerals are constants.

Baseline — what ships today

Toggle expands to nine ~95 px rows. Each row: italic gold Roman numeral · serif title · dotted leader filling the column · tabular page number. ~850 px total height; the leaders read as filler at any width over ~600 px.

I — Compact ledger

Tight single-line rows, no dotted leaders

The leaders are filler. Strip them out, halve the row padding, replace the page number with a small monospace doc-ref tag, and the nine entries fit in roughly a third of today's height. Every third row drops a darker hairline so the eye still has a rhythm without the leaders doing the work.

vs today — ~850 px → ~310 px. Rows go from 95 px to ~38 px. Loses leaders + page numerals; adds a discreet GV-A…GV-I doc-ref tag that ties to the audit register the rest of the dossier already uses.
Surface
Cream rows with hairline dividers; no card frame; every third hairline reads slightly darker (--hairline-2).
Active
Hover ramps row to gold ink + 8 % gold wash; click → scroll + close.
Mobile
Same layout; numeral column shrinks 36 → 30 px, label drops to 16 px.

II — Phased trio

Three groups of three: Orientation, Substance, Evidence

Nine flat rows pretend every chapter weighs the same. Group them the way the engagement actually moves — Orientation (About, Investment, Benefits) → Substance (Eligibility, Documents, Process) → Evidence (Visa-Free, Case Studies, Licenses) — and suddenly the contents page tells the reader what the dossier is for, not just where to click.

vs today — ~850 px → ~220 px on desktop. Same nine anchors, but framed by three editorial phase headers that double as anchor-able micro-landmarks. Mobile collapses to a single column with the same three headings.
Surface
Three columns separated by hairlines; phase header in italic Cormorant + monospace tag (I–III / IV–VI / VII–IX).
Active
Hover lifts the chapter to gold; phase headers are non-interactive labels, not links.
Mobile
Stacks vertically ≤ 760 px; phase headers stay, hairlines flip from vertical to horizontal.

III — Audit doc-control register

Five tabular columns, matched to the page's audit register

The freshness badge and the breadcrumb already use a doc-control register (HOST · STATUS · OPEN · CLOSES). The contents disclosure should join that same conversation rather than dropping a print appendix into the middle of an audit document. Columns: § · CHAPTER · DOC-REF · REVISED · STATUS.

vs today — ~850 px → ~360 px. Same nine entries, presented as a filing reference rather than a TOC. Aligns the disclosure with the breadcrumb's .ht-breadcrumb__doc-control pattern that already ships.
Surface
Five mono columns under a small-caps header row; italic Cormorant numerals + chapter titles; green dot for ON-PAGE.
Active
Hover wash (gold 7 %); click → scroll + close. Row is one <a>.
Mobile
Header hides; each row reflows to a 2-line stack: numeral + chapter + status on top, doc-ref + revised on the line below.

IV — Editorial running text

One standfirst paragraph, nine inline links

Resist the appendix completely. Render the contents as a single editorial standfirst — a magazine-style "in this issue" paragraph where the chapter titles are the inline links. Reads as advisory copy first, navigation second, and tells the reader the shape of the dossier without making them parse a list.

vs today — ~850 px → ~210 px. Loses the explicit list affordance — the trade is that the disclosure now says something rather than just enumerates. Drop-cap + chapter titles in italic Cormorant + small Roman numerals beside each linked title preserve the editorial register.
Surface
Single Cormorant 22 px paragraph; gold drop-cap; soft highlight under each link until hover lifts it.
Active
Hover removes the highlight wash and lifts the title to gold ink; click → scroll + close.
Mobile
Same paragraph reflows; type drops to 19 px, drop-cap to 46 px; small footer rule keeps the doc-control tone.

V — Stepper journey bar

Nine connected nodes in a single horizontal row, journey register

The dossier is a sequence — there's a reason "About" is before "Process". Show that. A single horizontal stepper, nine nodes connected by a hairline, italic Roman numeral inside each node, chapter title beneath. Dense, diagrammatic, and quietly signals "this is a path, not a menu."

vs today — ~850 px → ~150 px (one row). Trades vertical real-estate for horizontal — works because the labels are short. Adds an at-a-glance current-position indicator (filled ink node + gold dot) the existing list can't carry.
Surface
Nine 34 px nodes on a hairline track; italic Cormorant numerals inside; chapter titles below each node, monospace 5/9 progress tag.
Active
Hover lifts the node 2 px, fills it gold; current chapter is rendered as ink-filled with gold dot. Click → scroll + close.
Mobile
Track keeps its 760 px min-width and scrolls horizontally inside the disclosure — preserves the single-row gesture rather than collapsing to a list.

VI — Drop-cap magazine TOC

Big Fraunces italic numerals as drop-caps, two-up cells

Make the numerals do all the visual work. 56 px Fraunces italic Roman numerals, drop-cap large, paired with a Cormorant chapter title and a small monospace doc-ref. Two-column grid; the ninth chapter spans the bottom row as a deliberate punctuation. Reads like a luxury monthly's "in this issue" spread.

vs today — ~850 px → ~470 px. Heavier than the compact ledger but unmistakably editorial — the numerals carry the surface, the labels read second. Removes leaders + page-number column entirely.
Surface
Two columns × four rows + a full-width ninth cell; hairline grid; 56 px Fraunces italic numerals to the left of each label.
Active
Hover wash (gold 8 %) + numeral flips from gold to ink — keeps the label gold-quiet so the numeral leads the gesture.
Mobile
Single column, numerals shrink to 44 px; ninth cell stops being special.

VII — Marginalia rail

Italic standfirst on the left, numbered margin notes on the right

Two columns. The left holds an italic standfirst — what the dossier is and who it's for; the right is the chapter list rendered as marginalia: tiny gold em-rule, italic numeral, Cormorant title, monospace doc-ref. Reads as a published document with a reader's apparatus, not a navigation menu.

vs today — ~850 px → ~340 px. Adds a column of advisory copy where today there is dead space; the chapters become the apparatus that supports the reading. Pulls the spine's editorial register into the disclosure itself.
Surface
Two-column grid divided by a 1 px hairline; left column carries a soft 4 % gold wash; right is the navigable list.
Active
Hover lifts each chapter to gold and ramps its bottom hairline to gold; em-rule beside the numeral stays gold throughout.
Mobile
Stacks vertically ≤ 760 px — standfirst on top, list beneath; the rule rotates to a horizontal divider.
