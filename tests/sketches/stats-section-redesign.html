Sketch · stats-section-redesign

Five redesigns for the 14 / 100 / 32 stats strip.

Reading the screenshot. Three editorial pull-stats with footnote markers († ‡ §) and a centred bracketed [ Audit trail on request → ] button below. The information is good and the typography is right — but the layout has three concrete problems.

What's broken (per the brief).
  1. The whole strip doesn't sit as a section. Three columns of italic serif body copy run together; the eye has no anchor between numeral, claim and source, and the byline (— R.A.) repeats three times within a few centimetres.
  2. The footnote glyphs are different sizes. †, ‡ and § are three unrelated glyphs at three unrelated optical weights — they read as decoration, not as a key, and there's no marginalia/footer block to land on.
  3. The audit-trail button is in the wrong place. A bracketed serif pill centred under three columns reads as an aside, not as the section's primary tell-the-rest affordance. The brief calls for it on the right-hand side.

Every redesign below holds the data and voice constant — the three numerals, the three editorial claims and the — R.A. byline don't change. What changes is the surface: how the three claims relate, how (or whether) the footnote system survives, and where the audit-trail CTA lands.

I · Editorial pull-quote
Creative

Three pull-quotes, three identical gold seals.

Numerals dominate at 96 px italic; the editorial claim becomes a short italic pull-quote ending in — R.A. The mismatched † ‡ § glyphs are replaced by three identical 14 px circular gold seals carrying the letters a / b / c — same shape, same size, same colour — and the footnote text lands in a discreet three-column band at the foot of the section. Section masthead carries the audit-trail link on the right.

II · Stripped editorial
Minimalistic

Cut the footnotes. Three stats, one masthead, one link.

The boldest fix: remove the footnote system entirely. The three claims aren't long enough to warrant references on the page; the audit-trail link is the catch-all "show your working" affordance. No card, no border, no plus-disc — three editorial cells separated by air on cream. The Audit trail link sits as an italic right-aligned tagline in the section masthead.

III · Doc-control register
Professional

Restate the three stats as filing references.

Reuses the audit-report DNA from .ht-breadcrumb__doc-control and the program-page front-matter. Each stat becomes a row in a tabular ledger (REF · METRIC · DETAIL · SOURCE), with a monospace REF code replacing the decorative footnote glyph and a green-dot CURRENT indicator on the source cell. The Audit trail button sits in the table header on the right — same monospace register as the column headings, so it reads as part of the ledger.

IV · Marginalia journal
Editorial / longform — mine #1

Stacked rows with a marginalia rail on the right.

Three horizontal rows instead of three columns — gives each stat a full editorial line and stops the three columns of italic running together. A 40 px marginalia rail on the right holds a uniformly-sized a / b / c reference plus a small-caps source line (CFA · 2012 etc.), so the footnote system stays but every marker is the same glyph at the same size. Audit-trail returns to the top-right of the section masthead.

V · Almanac plate
Dossier / pitch-card — mine #2

Three hairline-framed plates with corner ticks.

Borrows the dossier-frame language of the site's .ht-pitch-card — each stat sits in its own hairline plate with gold corner ticks and a monospace REF chip at the top. The footnote glyphs disappear entirely (the REF chip carries the citation), and each plate closes with a hairline-divided foot row holding — R.A. and a small-caps source line. The Audit trail CTA is a right-aligned gold-outline ghost button in the section masthead — the brief register, not editorial.

Five drafts. Hold the copy and voice constant; only the surface, the citation system and the CTA placement change. My pick depends on context — III (Doc-control) if the section needs to feel like an audit firm, IV (Marginalia) if it needs to feel like an editorial column, I (Pull-quote) if you want to keep the column layout but fix the three bugs in place.
