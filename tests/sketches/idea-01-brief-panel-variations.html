Five ways to land the "Brief a Partner" panel.

Idea 01 is the right move — give the hero a conversion path without resorting to Astons' shouty red form. But "small panel anchored right" is one of many shapes it can take. Below, five genuinely distinct patterns. Pick one, A/B two, or layer them by breakpoint.

Baseline (the original 01): right-anchored glass card on the hero, three fields (you / jurisdiction / situation), gold "Send brief" button, microcopy "NDA-first · response in 48h · no marketing." Each variation below trades something against that baseline — different friction, different intent, different visual weight.
Variation 01·A

An inline conversational strip directly under the tagline.

No card, no panel — just a single rounded textarea sitting under the tagline like a private-message field. Placeholder reads "Tell us the situation in one sentence…" Gold arrow button on the right. Submitting opens a brief modal for name + email. Lower friction than three fields, conversational instead of formal.

Trade vs baseline: faster to start typing, less qualifying data up front. Best when the bottleneck is "they bounced before reaching the contact page," worst when the inbox needs pre-qualified leads.

01·A — One field, no card. Reads like a DM, not a form.

Variation 01·B

An inverted offer — give the dossier, ask for an email.

Flip the conversion logic. Instead of asking the visitor to brief us, we offer something tangible: "Get our 4-page private read on Portugal — minimums, real timelines, what we'd actually advise. Drop your email; the dossier and a calendar link arrive in your inbox." One field, big gold button, secondary link to schedule directly.

Trade vs baseline: commits us to producing the dossier (good content hygiene anyway), and inverts the energy — we're being generous, they're not yet committing. Probably the highest-converting variant; best for top-of-funnel traffic.

01·B — Inverts the ask. Probably the highest-converting variant.

Variation 01·C

A collapsed pill that expands into the full panel on tap.

Hero stays editorial-pure: just title, tagline, gold-rule. A small gold-bordered pill sits in the top-right corner — "Brief a Partner ↗". Click and it expands gracefully into the full three-field card (or animates open as a side drawer). Conversion path is one tap away without ever competing with the typography.

Trade vs baseline: you keep the cleanest possible hero, at the cost of one extra click. Probably the best fit if our north-star metric is "hero looks like an FT longread, not a landing page."

01·C — Collapsed pill (left) → expanded card (right). Cleanest hero.

Variation 01·D

A dock-on-scroll advisor card — appears once the hero is past.

Hero stays absolutely clean. The conversion path arrives later — once the user has scrolled past the hero, a compact card slides in from the bottom-right with a small partner portrait and one line: "Working on Portugal? Brief Filip directly →" Stays docked through the rest of the page. Marries idea 01 and idea 04 (named partner) into one element.

Trade vs baseline: the hero is pristine, but the conversion only appears for engaged readers. Skips drive-by traffic on purpose — fine if our buyer reads before they convert.

01·D — Conversion path docks in only when the reader is engaged.

Variation 01·E

A calendar-first panel — three real slots, no form.

Skip the form entirely. The card lists three actually-available slots from a partner's calendar — "Thu 7 May · 14:00 CET / Fri 8 May · 09:30 CET / Mon 11 May · 11:00 CET" — each tile clickable. Picking one opens a name+email modal and books the call. "Or pick another time →" link below as the escape hatch.

Trade vs baseline: zero typing to start; signals "we're real advisors with calendars, not a CRM." Costs us partner-time visibility and a Cal.com / Google integration. Best signal of quality bar; worst if a slot books with a tire-kicker.

01·E — Calendar slots replace the form. Maximum signal of quality bar.

How to choose: 01·A and 01·E minimise typing; 01·B inverts the offer and likely converts highest; 01·C protects the typography; 01·D respects the reader. Worth shipping two and A/B-testing — start with the baseline (current 01) vs 01·B.
