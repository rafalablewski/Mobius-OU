Ten redesigns of the "Pick a 30-minute slot" calendar.

Brief: rethink the booking card on the program page (src/components/BriefPanel.jsx), drawing on visual languages that get scheduling right — Apple's calendar surfaces, Tesla's in-vehicle UI, Lucid's interior-cluster restraint, Adobe's professional dock panels. Each idea below is a live HTML/CSS render sitting on the same dark hero ground the component lives on today, so we can compare without leaving the page.

Today. Cream card on dark hero. Eyebrow / serif title / three uniform slot rows / serif italic alt-link / hairline rule / uppercase microcopy. Reads like a private memo. The redesigns aim to keep that editorial register while borrowing the interaction patterns of these four product families.

Idea 01

Apple Stocks — frosted card, weekday chips.

Inspired by Apple — Stocks / Calendar widget

The iOS Stocks widget reads as a quiet, pre-rendered surface — frosted background, confident sans, soft chips you can tap without ceremony. Repurpose that for booking: each row is a weekday, the times for that day sit as three pill chips, the soonest is filled solid. A single tap selects, no second screen.

vs. today: we drop the serif title in favour of SF-style sans, and we let the visitor see more options at a glance (3 days × 3 times = 9 slots) without making the card taller — chips are denser than rows.

Idea 02

Apple Wallet — a perforated booking pass.

Inspired by Apple — Wallet / boarding pass

Wallet treats a single piece of information — a boarding time, a coffee balance — as something almost ceremonial. Use the same metaphor for the next slot: top stub shows the date in big serif numerals, perforated rule, bottom stub holds the actual time and an "Add to Apple Wallet"-feeling primary action. Two further options sit as secondary chips in the lower stub.

vs. today: commits to a single recommended time instead of three equal-weight rows. Better for "I'll trust your scheduling" visitors; worse for visitors who want to compare. The two micro-alternates partly hedge that.

Idea 03

Tesla touchscreen — slab, oversized time, single CTA.

Inspired by Tesla — vehicle UI (charging / nav)

Tesla's centre console reduces every interaction to a hierarchy: one giant primary state, three or four neighbour states, one CTA. The booking parallel is obvious — show the soonest slot at car-charger scale, three alternates as small slabs, and a single full-width "Reserve" button. Pure black, no decoration, no serifs.

vs. today: abandons the editorial cream card entirely. Instead the booking surface looks like a piece of professional software running inside the private memo — a deliberate genre clash that says "this is the working part of the page."

Idea 04

Tesla service — appointment list with a "soonest" tag.

Inspired by Tesla — Service appointment / Mobile app

Tesla's service-appointment screen and the "Schedule Service" flow lean on a monogram circle, a tight three-row appointment list, a faint outline CTA. Adapt that exactly: monogram of the host, a row per slot with day numerals on the left and a "Soonest" tag on the top row, alternates secondary, primary CTA as a pill outline.

vs. today: introduces a personal anchor (Filip's monogram) the current card lacks. The "Soonest" tag also explicitly ranks the slots, which today's card avoids — that's a stance to test against the editorial register.

Idea 05

Lucid cluster — three nodes on a horizontal hairline.

Inspired by Lucid Motors — Glass Cockpit / dashboard cluster

Lucid's dashboard arranges status across one continuous hairline rail with floating nodes — speed, charge, drive mode all read as points on a single horizon. Build the booker the same way: three slots become three pips on a champagne hairline, ultra-thin numerals at 38px, the soonest pip glowing. Wider format than the current card.

vs. today: rotates the layout from vertical stack to horizontal rail. Better fit on desktop hero with width to spare; needs a separate stacked treatment on mobile. Reads as instrumentation more than as memo, which is a deliberate signal of precision.

Idea 06

Lucid Air interior — champagne monogram + glass blades.

Inspired by Lucid Motors — Air cabin interior

The Lucid Air cabin is composed of vertical glass blades, a champagne palette and a central focal point. Translate to a booker: monogram of the host at the top, three glass-blade slot panels below, hairline gold separators between blades, no backgrounds — only a soft dome glow at the top. Reads as cabin instrumentation more than as a form.

vs. today: centres everything (today is left-aligned), and trades "card on dark" for "instrument cluster on dark" — the booker stops looking like a document and starts looking like a piece of equipment.

Idea 07

Adobe Creative Cloud — panel with swatches and shortcuts.

Inspired by Adobe — Creative Cloud panels / Spectrum

Adobe panels speak to professionals: a header with a brand square, a tight title row, a list of items with thin colour-swatch indicators on the left, monospace for any technical value, and keyboard shortcuts visible on every row. Reframe each booking slot as an item in that list — colour swatch by urgency, JetBrains Mono time, ⌥1/⌥2/⌥3 accelerators on hover.

vs. today: shifts genre from "editorial memo" to "professional tool." Useful if Mobius wants to feel operational rather than advisory; risky if the rest of the program page is leaning hard into the dossier voice.

Idea 08

Adobe XD — split month grid + slot dock.

Inspired by Adobe — XD / After Effects panel docks

XD's panel docks pair a navigator on one side and a list on the other, separated by a thin gridline. For booking: left pane is a small month grid (only weekdays with available slots are coloured), right pane is the day's three time slots — each with a monospace time and a kbd shortcut. Visitors who want to compare days do it on the left; visitors who want to book do it on the right.

vs. today: dramatically expands surface area — this is a panel, not a pull-quote. Probably too heavy for the program-page hero, but well-fitted to a dedicated /book route or a click-through state from the lighter cards.

Idea 09

Apple × Lucid — clock-face dial picker.

Inspired by Apple — Watch faces · Lucid — instrument dials

A literal clock face. The dial shows three pips at 09:30, 11:00 and 14:00, the hand rests on the currently-selected one, and the centre of the face shows the time in huge thin numerals. A pill row underneath flips between the next three weekdays so the visitor can scan a whole week without leaving the dial. White CTA at the bottom.

vs. today: the boldest departure — turns scheduling into a piece of horology. Risky on accessibility (the dial is decorative; the pills do the real work) and on novelty cost (visitors must learn the metaphor). But it's the most distinctive and the most "house style."

Idea 10

Tesla × Adobe — month grid + day-rail split panel.

Inspired by Tesla — dark UI · Adobe — split-panel docks

A more product-like cousin of idea 08. Left half is a Tesla-dark month grid with gold pips marking days where Filip has openings; right half is the rail of slots for the selected day, including a "BOOKED" tag style for the unavailable times so the visitor can see how busy the host is — earned credibility via density.

vs. today: shows scarcity. Today's card hides booked times entirely; this version makes capacity legible. It also pairs the editorial gold accent with a Tesla-grade neutral surface, so the brand voice survives despite the genre shift.
