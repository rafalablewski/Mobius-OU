Ten ways to redesign the expanded contents disclosure

Today's expanded "Contents · 9 chapters" disclosure is enormous — each row eats ~95 px, the dotted leader stretches across a full desktop column, and the result reads as a print appendix dropped into a webpage. Ten redesigns below, ranging from the most restrained (pure typography) to the most diagrammatic (stepper bar). All keep the same nine anchors and the same toggle affordance — only the surface of the open state changes.

Behavioural contract — held constant across all ten: the disclosure toggle (Contents · 9 chapters · +) stays as-is. Click any chapter entry → smooth-scroll to that chapter's id; toggle closes after navigation. Nine entries always visible when open — no overflow, no scrolling within the disclosure. The disclosure renders inside .ht-spine__contents on the cream spine background; gold accents and italic Cormorant numerals are constant.

Toggle expands to nine ~95 px rows. Each row: italic gold Roman numeral · serif title · dotted leader fills the column · tabular page number. ~850 px total height; the leaders read as filler in anything wider than ~600 px.

I — 3×3 letterpress grid

Nine cells, three columns, no card frames

Lose the leaders, lose the per-row stripe — pack the nine chapters into a 3×3 grid drawn with hairlines. Each cell carries the italic Roman numeral, the title, and the page number; the grid itself handles separation. Compact (~180 px tall), reads as a dossier index, not a print appendix.

vs today — ~850 px → ~180 px. Loses the dotted leaders (which are filler at desktop widths anyway). Keeps the editorial type pairing.
Surface
3 columns × 3 rows; 1 px hairline grid in --hairline; no card lift.
Active
Hover fills the cell with rgba(gold, 0.08); click → scroll + close.
Mobile
Collapses to 2 columns ≤ 768 px, 1 column ≤ 480 px.

II — Two-column dense list

Same anatomy, doubled up

The most conservative redesign: keep the row of italic numeral, title, short dotted leader and tabular page — but split into two columns of five (with the ninth filling the second slot of the last row). Half the height for free; the leader becomes meaningful again because the column is narrower.

vs today — ~850 px → ~280 px. Same vocabulary as the current row, dotted leader returns to its proper, narrow size.
Surface
Two equal columns with 32 px gutter; rows separated by hairlines top + bottom.
Active
Hover ramps the title to gold; click → scroll + close.
Mobile
Collapses to one column ≤ 600 px (back to original ladder).

narrow column → leader does its job

III — Inline pill row

Italic chip cloud, one wrap-line tall

Treat the chapter list as a chip nav — nine pills wrapping in a flex row, each carrying italic numeral and serif title. ~80 px tall on desktop, two wrap-lines on tablet, three on narrow phones. The quietest, most navigation-shaped option of the ten.

vs today — ~850 px → ~80 px. Loses the page numbers (chips don't really earn them); reads as quick-jump nav, not as a print TOC.
Surface
Cream pills with hairline border; flex-wrap reflows naturally.
Active
Hover ramps border to gold and tints background; current-chapter pill is filled gold + ink text.
Mobile
Same row, just more wrap.

nav-shaped — wraps to fit any width

IV — Stepper bar

Nine connected nodes — a journey, not a list

Re-read the chapter sequence as a process. A 1 px gold rule across the disclosure, with nine ringed nodes evenly spaced; italic Roman numeral above each, italic chapter label below. Reads as a process indicator — closer to the doc-control register on the program page than to a print TOC. Roughly 130 px tall.

vs today — ~850 px → ~130 px. Reframes the chapters from "table of contents" to "stages in a dossier" — supports the program page's narrative arc more than its appendix-ness.
Surface
9-column grid; centred gold rule passes through each node; node fills on hover.
Active
Hover ramps node to gold-fill and label to gold-deep; click → scroll + close.
Mobile
Becomes vertical at ≤ 700 px (rule rotates 90°, nodes stack).

V — Audit doc-control table

Five mono columns: chapter as filing reference

Reuse the program page's existing doc-control register. A monospace five-column table — NUM · CHAPTER · DOC-REF · PG · STATUS — with green-dot status pips. Treats every chapter like a filed document, which the rest of the page already does for the program itself. Most institutional / least decorative.

vs today — ~850 px → ~360 px. Adds a doc-ref column (e.g. SEC-I/01) and a status indicator that the current row doesn't carry; lands the disclosure in the same audit-report register as the freshness badge round-three options.
Surface
Tabular grid in JetBrains Mono 10.5 px caps; serif italic numeral + serif chapter title interleaved with mono refs.
Active
Hover tints the row; current chapter status reads ON-PAGE (green dot), others READY.
Mobile
Doc-ref column hides ≤ 600 px; status pip stays.

filing reference, not appendix

VI — Editorial running text

One italic standfirst paragraph

Read like a magazine: chapters strung together as a single italic Cormorant paragraph, each title an underline-on-hover link, with gold dot separators between them. "I About · II Investment · III Benefits …" Most editorial register; under 100 px.

vs today — ~850 px → ~80 px. Drops the print-TOC apparatus entirely (no leaders, no page numbers, no per-row stripe). Closest cousin to a magazine's "in this issue" line.
Surface
Single Cormorant 19 px paragraph; italic numerals in gold-deep, titles inherit ink, gold dots between.
Active
Hover ramps title colour to gold-deep + underlines with a gold rule; click → scroll + close.
Mobile
Same paragraph, naturally wraps to 3–5 lines.
